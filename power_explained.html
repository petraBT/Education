Understanding Electrical Power

$P = I \times V$

What is Electrical Power?

In simple terms, power is the rate at which work is done or energy is used. A giant stadium floodlight is far more "powerful" than a dim nightlight because it uses energy at a much faster rate.

The equation for electrical power, $P = I \times V$, is the key. To understand it, we need to know what $I$ and $V$ are:

Voltage (V)

Think of this as electrical pressure or the "push." It's the force that makes electricity want to move.

Current (I)

Think of this as the electrical flow rate. It's the amount of electricity that is actually moving past a point per second.

You cannot have power with just one or the other. You need both the push (Voltage) and the flow (Current).

Tying It All Back Together

Electrical Power ($P = IV$) is the same idea. It's the combination of the electrical "push" (Voltage) and the actual "flow" (Current). Look at these real-world examples:

Small Phone Charger

Uses the wall's "push" (Voltage) but only allows a tiny "flow" (Current).

$120V \times 0.1A = $ 12 Watts

Low Power

Large Electric Stove

Uses the same "push" (Voltage) but draws a huge "flow" (Current) to get hot.

$120V \times 20A = $ 2,400 Watts

High Power
